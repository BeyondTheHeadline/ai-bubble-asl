Every interest rate on one ladder, from the Fed to your mortgage — English transcript (ASL Short)

Short answer: Interest rates sit on a ladder. At the bottom is the federal funds rate—the very short-term rate on overnight loans between banks. That can influence Treasury bills, money-market rates, and other short-term borrowing; then bank business loans, adjustable-rate loans, some credit-card rates, and car financing; and farther up, business bonds, long-term government bonds, and home mortgages.

Full English transcript

English captions for Every interest rate on one ladder, from the Fed to your mortgage (Beyond the Headline / @BeyondTheHeadLineASL). Timestamps follow the published video.

Remember the ladder with the different levels of interest rates? At the bottom is the federal funds rate. That's the very short-term rate on overnight loans between banks.

That can influence rates above it: Treasury bills. Money-market rates. And other short-term borrowing rates.

Those can influence rates farther up: Bank business loans. Adjustable-rate loans. Some credit-card rates.

Car financing. And those can influence rates farther up: Business bonds. Long-term government bonds.

And home mortgages. And eventually, all of that influences the actual decisions people and businesses make. Do I buy a car?

Do I buy a house? Should my business open a new location? Should we hire more people?
