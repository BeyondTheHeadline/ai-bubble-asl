The Fed's rate is really an overnight loan between banks — English transcript (ASL Short)

Short answer: Imagine one bank ends the day with plenty of reserves at the Fed that it doesn't need, and another ends the day short. Banks can borrow those reserves from one another through very short-term loans—typically overnight. The interest rate on that loan between banks is the federal funds rate: the rate for which the Fed just announced its target.

FAQ

What is the federal funds rate, really?
It is the interest rate on a very short-term loan of reserves between banks. Typically a bank borrows the money overnight and then pays it back. That is the rate for which the Fed just announced its target. Watch this part
Why would one bank borrow from another overnight?
One bank may reach the end of the day with plenty of reserves left at the Fed that it doesn't need, while another doesn't have enough and decides it needs to borrow. Banks can borrow reserves from one another through those overnight loans. Watch this part

Full English transcript

English captions for The Fed's rate is really an overnight loan between banks (Beyond the Headline / @BeyondTheHeadLineASL). Timestamps follow the published video.

So now, what does the federal funds rate actually do? Imagine one bank reaches the end of the day and has plenty of reserves left at the Fed that it doesn't need. Another bank reaches the end of the day and doesn't have enough.

That bank decides it needs to borrow money. Banks can borrow reserves from one another through very short-term loans. Typically, a bank borrows the money overnight and then pays it back.

The interest rate on that type of loan between banks is the federal funds rate. That is the rate for which the Fed just announced its target.
